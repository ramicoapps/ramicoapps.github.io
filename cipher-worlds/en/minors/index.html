Children & Parents

Updated 8.11.2026

Version 1.0 · Effective date: 8.11.2026

This page is for parents and guardians. It explains how Cipher Worlds: Morse treats children, what we do and do not collect, how ads work for younger players, and how you can control purchases and delete data. It adds to our Privacy Policy.

The game is made by RamiCoApps. Contact: ramicoapps@gmail.com.

1. Who the game is for

Cipher Worlds: Morse teaches Morse code and is made for a mixed audience: children and adults. Because children play it, we have designed it to collect as little as possible from everyone.

2. The neutral age screen

The first time the game starts – before any ad is requested – it asks "What year were you born?" with a list of years.

  • No year is pre-selected, the screen does not hint at any age limit, and no answer gives a reward or unlocks anything.
  • The game keeps only the resulting age group on the device: under 13, 13–15, or 16 or older. The year of birth is not stored and not sent anywhere.
  • If the question is not answered, the game does not request ads.
  • The age group cannot be changed inside the game except with Settings → Reset everything, which deletes progress and asks the question again.

3. What younger players get

Age groupAds
Under 13Ad requests are marked as child-directed and under the age of consent; only G-rated, non-personalized ads.
13–15Ad requests are marked as under the age of consent; only G-rated, non-personalized ads.
16+G-rated ads; personalized ads only where Google's consent tools allow it, otherwise non-personalized.

"Non-personalized" means the ads are not based on the player's past behaviour. Ads are provided by Google AdMob, which is listed by Google Play as a Families Self-Certified Ads SDK. Google still receives some technical information (such as IP address and device information) when an ad is shown, for delivery, measurement and fraud prevention – see section 5 of our Privacy Policy.

Ads appear between some levels (never with Premium). Rewarded ads are optional: the player chooses to watch one for a hint, a chest or extra tries.

4. What the game does not have

  • No accounts and no sign-up. The game never asks for a name, e-mail, phone, address, photo or location, and does not use the camera or microphone.
  • No chat, no messages, no user-generated content, no sharing between players.
  • No real friends lists – Pip's friends are characters in the story.
  • No contests with real prizes, no subscriptions.
  • No analytics or crash-reporting tools, and no server of ours. Progress stays on the device.

The only optional link to an online account is Google Play Games saved games on Android, which happens only if the player chooses to sign in (see section 7 of the Privacy Policy). It is not offered to players under 13: their progress stays on the device (backup files still work).

5. Purchases and how to prevent accidental ones

The game sells, through the stores only, a one-time Premium upgrade and packs of hints, coins and diamonds. Coins and diamonds are virtual, have no cash value, and everything in the game can also be earned by playing. All payments go through Google Play or Apple; we never see payment details.

The shop also has mystery boxes: a random cosmetic item for coins or diamonds. The chance of each rarity is shown before a box is opened, and the items only change how things look. Since diamonds can be bought with money, you may want to talk about the boxes with your child and use the purchase controls below.

We recommend that parents turn on the stores' own controls:

Google Play (Android)

  • Purchase verification: in the Play Store settings, open _Purchase verification_ and require verification for all purchases. The menu and options differ by region. See Purchase verification for Google Play.
  • Family Link: if your child's account is managed with Family Link, you can require your approval for purchases (all content, paid content, or in-app purchases only). See Purchase approvals on Google Play.

Apple (iPhone/iPad)

  • Ask to Buy: with Family Sharing, the organizer or a parent/guardian can require approval for a child's purchases. See Ask to Buy.
  • Screen Time: you can block in-app purchases in _Settings → Screen Time_ (the exact menu depends on the iOS version). See Turn off in-app purchases with Screen Time.

Refunds. Refunds are handled by the store that charged you (Google Play or Apple's "Report a Problem"). Google Play, for example, accepts refund requests directly within a limited time after purchase – see Request a refund on Google Play. We cannot refund store charges ourselves, but write to us and we will help – for example if a purchase was not delivered.

6. Laws and store rules we take into account

We describe here how we approach these rules. This is not a statement that a regulator or store has reviewed or certified the game.

  • United States – COPPA. The Children's Online Privacy Protection Rule (16 CFR Part 312) protects children under 13. The FTC amended the Rule in 2025 (published in the Federal Register on 22 April 2025), and the amendments include separate parental consent for disclosing children's information to third parties for targeted advertising. Our approach: the game does not ask children for personal information; it uses a neutral age screen; for players under 13 ads are requested as child-directed and non-personalized, so they are not targeted. We do not collect personal information from children ourselves.
  • European Union / UK – GDPR. Under Article 8 of the GDPR, where an online service relies on consent, a child's consent is valid from age 16, and each EU country may set a lower age, not below 13. In the UK the age is 13. Because the age differs by country, the game treats every player under 16 as under the age of consent and shows them only non-personalized ads; it does not ask children to give consent for personalized ads.
  • Israel – Protection of Privacy Law, 5741-1981 (including Amendment 13). The law has no specific children's-consent age; parents or guardians may act for their children under the general law.
  • Google Play – Families Policy. Apps whose target audience includes children must follow Google Play's Families Policy. For mixed-audience apps this includes a neutral age screen, ads for children only from Families Self-Certified Ads SDKs, no personalized or interest-based ads for children, and not transmitting the advertising ID and similar identifiers from children or users of unknown age.
  • Apple – App Store. Because the game is for a mixed audience and is not designed only for children, we do not list it in the App Store Kids Category. Apple's guideline 5.1.4 still requires apps that may be used by children to comply with children's privacy laws, which we aim to do as described above.

7. Your rights as a parent

You may contact us at ramicoapps@gmail.com to ask what information about your child we hold, or to ask us to correct or delete it. Because the game keeps your child's data only on the device and we have no accounts or server, we normally hold no information about your child; we will tell you so and help you use the steps below.

You can also complain to a data protection authority – in Israel, the Privacy Protection Authority; in the EEA, your national authority; in the UK, the ICO; in the US, the Federal Trade Commission.

8. How to reset or delete your child's data

  • On the device – Settings → Reset:
    • _Reset progress_ – deletes progress;
    • _Reset settings_ – deletes settings;
    • _Reset everything_ – deletes all progress and settings, including the age group, and starts again with the age question. The purchase record is kept so Premium still works.
  • Uninstall the game to delete the whole local database.
  • Google Play Games (if your child signed in): delete the game's saved data in the Play Games settings or at play.google.com/games/profile – see Delete your Play Games data.
  • Backup files: delete any exported backup file from wherever it was saved.
  • Advertising data held by Google: reset or delete the device's advertising ID, and use Google's ad settings – see How Google uses information from apps that use its services.

9. Questions

Write to ramicoapps@gmail.com. We will answer as soon as we reasonably can.
