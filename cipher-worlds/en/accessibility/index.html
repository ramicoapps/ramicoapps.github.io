Accessibility Statement

Updated 10.11.2026

1. Accessibility of the Game

It matters to RamiCoApps that Cipher Worlds: Morse (the "Game") be playable by as many people as possible, including people with disabilities. We work to improve its accessibility.

This statement describes the adjustments that exist in the Game today, and where it still falls short. No formal accessibility audit has been carried out. We do not claim that the Game conforms to Israeli Standard IS 5568 or to WCAG.

This statement is published voluntarily.

The Game is developed and operated by RamiCoApps. The adjustments were made in the Game itself, without an add-on or an external accessibility tool. We will update this statement when the Game's accessibility changes.

2. What was adapted

  • Screen readers (TalkBack / VoiceOver): buttons, icons and game elements have accessibility labels and roles, so a screen reader can describe them.
  • Dot and Dash buttons: when a screen reader is on, the Game automatically uses on-screen Dot and Dash buttons instead of the timed Morse key. Holding a key for a precise time is not possible with a screen reader. Rhythm-based questions are adapted accordingly.
  • Reduced motion: Settings → Animations lets you follow the device's "reduce motion" setting, or choose Reduced or Full. With reduced motion, effects such as confetti and spinning animations are turned off or simplified.
  • Text size: text grows with the device's font-size setting, up to a limit of about 130%, so the layout stays readable. Long text that does not fit its button or box is made slightly smaller to fit.
  • Colour contrast: pairs of text colour and background colour are checked in automated tests against the AA contrast level.
  • Sound, music, effects and vibration: each can be turned on or off separately, and the Morse tone volume can be adjusted.
  • Playback speed: in practice, challenges and the Code Map you can choose Slow, Normal or Fast. Levels use their own set speed.
  • Tap timing: you can tune the Morse key to your own tapping rhythm, or go back to the standard timing.
  • Languages: the interface is available in 7 languages: Hebrew, English, German, Spanish, French, Japanese and Portuguese. In Hebrew the layout is right-to-left.

3. Known limitations

  • Some questions rely on hearing Morse signals or on timing. The alternative ways below may not cover every case equally well.
  • Text scaling is capped, so very large font settings are not fully applied.
  • Some parts of the Game are highly visual, for example Pip's World and decorative animations. Not every image or decorative element is described to screen readers.
  • The Game has not been systematically tested with every screen reader, switch-access or external-keyboard setup. It has not yet been tested on iPhone or iPad.

4. Alternative ways

When sound is turned off, the Game shows the Morse signal on the screen instead of playing it, so many levels can be played without hearing. With a screen reader you can answer with Dot and Dash buttons instead of a timed key. If part of the Game is not accessible to you, you can contact us and we will try to find an alternative way.

5. Ads

The Game shows full-screen ads: between some levels, and optional "rewarded" ads you can choose to watch. The ads are provided by Google, a third party, and are not under our control, so they may not be accessible.

6. Contact about accessibility

Do you have difficulty using the Game, or an idea that would make it more accessible? We will be glad to hear about it. It helps to mention the problem, the screen, the type of device, and the screen reader or assistive tool you used.

RamiCoApps is a one-person business, so there is no separate accessibility coordinator. Requests are handled by the developer, at the address below, which is also the general contact address. We will do our best to reply and to help.

You can contact us by email: ramicoapps@gmail.com
